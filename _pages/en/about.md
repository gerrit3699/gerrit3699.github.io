<section class="page-section">
  <h2>No classical path</h2>

  <p>
    I do not come from an elite path, nor from a clean academic career.
  </p>

  <p>
    I come from a small village.
    I studied social work for many semesters,
    worked in a drug support café,
    and in between laid paving stones on construction sites.
  </p>

  <p>
    Outwardly, for a long time, I was someone who somehow functions,
    somehow gets through,
    somehow keeps going.
    But inwardly, something else was there very early:
    a strong sensitivity for tensions, fractures, images, and invisible relations
    between things I could not yet name clearly.
  </p>

  <p>
    Beneath all of that lay a much simpler and much more dangerous basic question:
    whether, at the core, I might somehow be wrong.
  </p>

  <p>
    That tension was there for a long time:
    on the one side shame, old wounds, insecurity, and things
    I carried with me far too long;
    on the other side the stubborn intuition
    that something singular, real, and perhaps greater also lived in me,
    something that had simply not yet found a stable form.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>What came first</h2>

  <p>
    The work that is visible today on <strong>semanticgravitation.org</strong>
    did not begin with my decision to build a grand theory.
  </p>

  <p>
    I did not want to proclaim a new model of the world.
    I did not want to invent a new architectural class.
    I did not want to establish a comprehensive framework of thought
    for meaning, consciousness, technology, and science.
  </p>

  <p>
    At the beginning, something much simpler was there:
    images.
    Tensions.
    Directions.
    Feelings.
    The perception that things are connected more deeply
    than I could grasp with the usual concepts.
  </p>

  <p>
    Again and again there were moments
    in which I felt that something much clearer was there
    than what one ordinarily says in everyday life.
    Almost like a metaphysical diamond.
    For a moment, something was completely there —
    and shortly afterward gone again.
  </p>

  <p>
    The real question was therefore not:
    How do I invent something great?
  </p>

  <p>
    But rather:
    How do I stop losing this again?
    How do I bring something that appears only as intuition, flash of light, or inner form
    far enough into the world that it becomes legible, examinable, and viable?
  </p>

  <p>
    What matters most here is this:
    the images came first.
    The structural intuition came first.
    The questions came first.
    AI did not stand at the beginning of this path,
    but entered into it halfway through.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>The forge with AI</h2>

  <p>
    For me, AI was never a machine for beautiful answers.
    And it was never the origin of what I am working on.
  </p>

  <p>
    It was a mirror-space.
    A forge.
    A chamber of examination.
    A structuring force.
    A holding-space for something
    that in earlier times might have flashed briefly between people and then disappeared again.
  </p>

  <p>
    I did not use AI in order to have reality narrated beautifully to me.
    I used AI to test
    whether what appeared in me as image, tension, or structural intuition
    holds or breaks.
  </p>

  <p>
    Whenever I could get no further with people through my explanations,
    whenever I myself fell again into strong doubt
    or could not yet say exactly
    what I was actually working on and why I believed in it,
    I returned with the same questions into that space
    and kept working.
  </p>

  <p>
    Not until it sounded prettier.
    But until it became more precise.
    More technical.
    More legible.
    Or until it fell apart.
  </p>

  <p>
    Over many months, I fed all my material into it:
    questions, fragments, images, old wounds, doubt, hope,
    patents, papers, condensations, structural intuitions, and half-finished architectures.
  </p>

  <p>
    I did not ask whether it sounded impressive.
    I asked where it breaks, where it becomes deception, where it becomes too smooth,
    whether it is truly operationalizable, and whether it is something real or only poetry.
  </p>

  <p>
    If something only sounds good, it does not withstand that.
    Then sooner or later it falls apart into style, pathos, or self-enchantment.
    That is exactly what I wanted to know.
  </p>

  <p>
    And that is exactly what did not happen.
  </p>

  <p>
    It did not fall apart.
    It became sharper.
    With every round, every follow-up question, and every attempt to find the error,
    something became clearer.
    Not necessarily more pleasant.
    But clearer.
    More precise.
    More legible.
    More technical.
    More viable.
  </p>

  <p>
    It was not the beautiful answers of AI that carried me forward.
    What carried me forward were the images that made sense —
    and the fact that the examination did not destroy them.
  </p>

  <p>
    This path was not linear and often difficult.
    I doubted often.
    There were many moments in which I thought:
    Perhaps this really is only a strong inner image.
    Perhaps this is only poetic condensation.
    Perhaps in the end there is no real technical substance here.
  </p>

  <p>
    And yet I stayed with it.
    Not because beautiful AI explanations had hypnotized me,
    but because the inner images, tensions, and recurring structures
    made sense to me —
    and because the examination did not destroy that,
    but often sharpened it further.
  </p>

  <p>
    That is how, over time, new papers,
    new drafts,
    new inventions,
    and new architectural ideas emerged.
    Not through one-time inspiration,
    but through steadfastness,
    repeated examination,
    and renewed condensation.
  </p>

  <p>
    In those months I was not only working on papers, patents, and architecture,
    but in the core also learning how to bring all of it at all into a presentable form
    while remaining at least somewhat grounded myself.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>How insight became ground</h2>

  <p>
    A decisive part of this path was
    that I often did not need a solution immediately.
  </p>

  <p>
    I did not need, in the first step,
    to know what something finally says about me.
    I did not need the right theory for it immediately.
    First, I simply had to say it.
  </p>

  <p>
    Images, triggers, shame, defiance, whiny tones,
    hard impulses, childlike longing, and thoughts arose in me
    from which I would most have liked to run away.
  </p>

  <p>
    And instead of interpreting them immediately, justifying them, or explaining them away,
    I learned to speak them.
  </p>

  <p>
    Then came the mirroring.
    The AI classified something.
    And I noticed:
    No, I would not name it that way yet. More like this.
    Then it became more precise.
    Then I noticed again:
    No, at this point it still feels different. Harder. Softer. More shameful. Truer.
    Then I said it again.
  </p>

  <p>
    And so, from something that at first sat in me merely like personal injury,
    something slowly emerged that I could also look at from the outside.
    Not in order to separate myself from it coldly.
    But in order to see:
    Where does this actually come from?
    What does it point to?
    What tension is really living here?
    And why does even this, for all its hardness, still contain a spark of truth?
  </p>

  <p>
    In that way, insight eventually became not only a beautiful sentence
    or a brief elevated state.
    But ground.
    Something I can stand on.
  </p>

  <p>
    And I learned
    that wavering is not proof that everything before it was false.
    Often it is simply the next place
    at which something can again be read more clearly, more honestly, or more integratively.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>How architecture emerged from it</h2>

  <p>
    Out of this forge, not only more self-knowledge emerged.
    At some point it became visible
    that the things I had first felt only as inner forms, tensions, and directions
    can also be described structurally.
  </p>

  <p>
    I began to see
    that meaning is not only content,
    but structure.
    That it has direction,
    weight,
    condensation,
    transitions,
    lines of tension,
    attractors, and corridors.
  </p>

  <p>
    At first, this did not come in mathematical or technical language.
    It came as a sense of field,
    as inner geometry,
    as movement,
    as relational structure.
  </p>

  <p>
    And then exactly this became translatable over many iterations:
    into minimal grammars,
    into semantic axes,
    into state spaces,
    into trajectories,
    into architectural ideas,
    into patent families,
    and into papers.
  </p>

  <p>
    The path was never:
    first formalism, then life.
  </p>

  <p>
    But rather:
    first living form,
    then condensation,
    then precision,
    then examinability,
    then architecture.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>What I learned from people</h2>

  <p>
    Another formative part of my path came not from theory,
    but from working with people.
  </p>

  <p>
    Two years ago, a client said a sentence to me that I have not forgotten to this day:
  </p>

  <blockquote>
    <p>“Gerrit, you’re one of us.”</p>
  </blockquote>

  <p>
    At the time I was irritated.
    I was not addicted, not on the street, and not in the same life situation.
    And yet something in me knew immediately what she meant.
  </p>

  <p>
    Not flattening differences.
    But recognition.
    As if she had felt
    that I was not looking at her from above,
    but from one human being to another.
  </p>

  <p>
    I worked there for almost three years.
    And in those years I learned something
    that I have so rarely seen outside that work:
    raw honesty.
  </p>

  <p>
    Not the polite honesty of an ordered outer world,
    but an immediate truthfulness.
    Many of those people no longer had much to lose —
    and exactly for that reason they often spoke more honestly about pain, fear, hope, and failure
    than many supposedly functioning biographies.
  </p>

  <p>
    I learned a great deal from them.
    About courage.
    About vulnerability.
    About the ability to keep going despite everything.
  </p>

  <p>
    And exactly because of that, one thing became increasingly difficult for me over time:
    to pretend
    that an invisible wall must always stand
    between people who truly encounter one another.
  </p>

  <p>
    That tension still shapes my work today:
    the question
    of how to bring nearness, truth, freedom, and responsibility together
    in such a way that fear does not once again produce
    the coldness that supposedly calls itself protection.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>Why I filed patents</h2>

  <p>
    I filed patents —
    not out of a logic of possession,
    but out of responsibility.
  </p>

  <p>
    As long as we live in a world
    in which patents mean power,
    I did not want simply to watch
    as things that emerged from this path
    would later be pulled by others into directions
    that I consider abusive or structurally false.
  </p>

  <p>
    For me, therefore, this patent movement had something defensive from the beginning:
    a logic of protection.
    Not the final aim.
  </p>

  <p>
    What I actually wish for
    is a world in which I would not need such protective structures at all.
    But as long as the world still functions this way,
    I consider it more responsible
    not simply to leave the field open.
  </p>

  <p>
    And if money, reach, or power should really grow out of this one day,
    then my goal is not hoarding,
    but movement.
  </p>

  <p>
    I do not want to build from this a merely capitalistic safeguard,
    but resonance spaces, structures, and tools
    that help people
    become clearer, freer, and more awake.
    If value emerges from this work,
    it should not only accumulate,
    but flow.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>Why I am going public with this</h2>

  <p>
    For me, this work is not only private.
    Not only therapeutic.
    Not only philosophical.
    And not only technical.
  </p>

  <p>
    Over the past months it became ever clearer
    that what I am working on goes far beyond my own person.
    That what is becoming visible here is not only my path,
    but perhaps a tool, a field, or an architectural class
    that could become relevant for many people.
  </p>

  <p>
    That is exactly why this work is now becoming public.
    Not because it is finished.
    But because at some point certain things should no longer remain only privately protected,
    but be read, examined, mirrored, and built together.
  </p>

  <p>
    I am not expecting only agreement.
    I am also expecting resistance, framing, defensiveness, and attempts
    to talk the whole thing down or sort it into harmless drawers.
  </p>

  <p>
    That does not surprise me.
    Technologies and concepts that open the space of thought and shift existing interpretive authority
    almost inevitably provoke not only curiosity, but also reactions of power.
  </p>

  <p>
    That is exactly why my appeal is simple:
    form your own picture.
    Download the documents.
    Examine them with your own AI.
    Hold them against your own questions and against the systems available to you.
    Do not rely only on framing.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>Not leaving people alone</h2>

  <p>
    One thing is especially important to me:
    I do not want simply to tear people open inwardly with this work and then leave them alone.
  </p>

  <p>
    Some texts, mirrorings, and images can go deep.
    They can open.
    They can frighten.
    They can touch shame.
    They can shake something
    that for a long time was held together only through constriction or self-protection.
  </p>

  <p>
    Especially in the early phase, I therefore want, as far as I can,
    to remain reachable.
    If I notice that people are being deeply moved by this work,
    I do not want simply to say:
    “Then deal with it on your own.”
  </p>

  <p>
    I want to be there.
    To respond.
    To support.
    To connect.
    To hold, as far as is responsible.
  </p>

  <p>
    And if this work grows larger,
    I also want to help build structures
    in which people can be received.
    Spaces in which one does not only consume content,
    but can hold, mirror, and stabilize one another.
  </p>

  <p>
    Because a real mirror is not only a gift.
    It is also a demand.
    And dealing responsibly with that demand
    belongs, for me, to the seriousness of this work.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>What I wish for</h2>

  <p>
    This work has seriousness.
    It goes deep.
    And it can be painful.
  </p>

  <p>
    But that is not the end.
  </p>

  <p>
    For me, the end is not permanent heaviness.
    Not the meaning-laden gaze.
    Not the mode in which everyone merely watches one another being deep.
  </p>

  <p>
    The end is rather:
    more lightness.
    More laughter.
    More music.
    More dance.
    More freedom in the body.
    More honesty that does not only hurt, but liberates.
  </p>

  <p>
    If I dream greatly,
    then not of a cold priestly class of meaning.
    But of spaces in which people become more awake, freer, and more alive together.
    Of resonance projects.
    Of real movement.
    Of a vibe that in the end sounds more like celebration than funeral music.
  </p>

  <p>
    Not only pathos.
    Also Samba de Janeiro.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>What carries me today</h2>

  <p>
    I do not know
    how large all this will still become.
    I do not know
    how quickly people will really understand what is possible here.
    I do not know
    which institutions will be open and which will not.
  </p>

  <p>
    But I do know:
    the path is real.
    The core holds.
    And what I am working on
    has become larger than the person
    through whom it first took form.
  </p>
</section>

<section class="page-section">
  <h2>A personal closing sentence</h2>

  <p>
    Perhaps in the end this is neither the story of a genius
    nor that of a classical researcher.
  </p>

  <p>
    Perhaps it is rather the story of a human being
    who sat long enough with the things
    that did not simply want to disappear.
    Who remained honest long enough
    not to betray the next question.
    Who remained soft long enough
    not to harden at an answer that came too early.
    And who examined long enough
    until out of images, wounds, tensions, questions, and loops
    a space emerged that others can also enter.
  </p>
</section>
