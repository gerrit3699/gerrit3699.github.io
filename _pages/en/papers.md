        <h2>Current status</h2>
      </div>

      <div class="section-body">
        <p>
          The papers, patents, and accompanying documents gathered here show
          the current theoretical and architectural state of <strong>Semantic Gravitation</strong>.
        </p>

        <p>
          What matters here is this:
          the central lines of the framework have so far <strong>not been experimentally validated</strong>.
          The current step is above all a public theoretical and structural drop:
          the publication of a framework
          that can now be deliberately examined, criticized, further specified, implemented,
          and — ideally in collaboration with partners — developed empirically.
        </p>

        <p>
          The papers and patents already exist as condensed theoretical and architectural elaborations.
          Experimental validation and concrete implementation, by contrast, still lie ahead.
        </p>

        <p>
          These documents are therefore not blueprints in a narrow mechanical sense,
          but neither are they merely loose principle sketches.
          They are better understood as <strong>dynamic construction frameworks</strong>:
          some foundational building blocks and operative elements are explicitly named,
          while many concrete expressions, parameters, and system forms
          will only emerge from the described relations, transitions, and dynamics.
        </p>

        <p>
          The patents, too, primarily mark operative directions and protective spaces
          that emerge from the theoretical framework.
          At the same time, it remains open
          to what extent individual lines at this level of abstraction
          will later hold up as concrete technical implementations,
          robust intellectual property,
          or actually realized systems.
        </p>

        onward to complex systems,
        and finally to the condensed overall structure of the global attractor,

      <h2>Patents</h2>

      <p>
        I am deliberately <strong>not providing a complete catalog</strong> here
        of all already filed protective spaces,
        but rather a selection of <strong>application examples and application architectures</strong>
        through which it becomes most visible
        how the framework of <strong>Semantic Gravitation</strong> could be used in practice.
      </p>

      <p>
        These examples are meant not only to say <em>that</em> applications could emerge from the theory,
        but above all <strong>how this might phenomenologically feel and practically affect the world</strong>
        if such systems were actually built.
      </p>

      <p>
        The patents did not arise from a pure logic of possession or monetization.
        They primarily serve a <strong>protective logic</strong>:
        an attempt to secure the underlying principles
        so that they are not prematurely appropriated by чуждые power or optimization logics,
        closed off,
        and perfected against their original meaning.
      </p>

      <p>
        The hope is
        to secure a framework in this way
        that — if it proves viable —
        remains as <strong>universal, open in its effects, and not merely privately exploitable</strong> as possible.
      </p>

      <p>
        What follows here are therefore first visible directions
        of a larger architectural class.
      </p>

        <h2>1. Edge Alignment — AI alignment with a backbone</h2>
      </div>

      <div class="section-body">
        <p>
          This application line shows what the framework could mean for the <strong>alignment of AI systems</strong>.
        </p>

        <p>
          Instead of tuning a system merely toward softened agreeableness, conditioning,
          or superficially harmless answers,
          the question here concerns the movement of a system in semantic space:
          whether transitions are viable,
          whether drift emerges,
          whether a conversation keeps its dignity,
          whether correction remains possible,
          and whether the system has <strong>more inner backbone</strong>.
        </p>

        <p>
          Phenomenologically, this would feel different from many AI systems today:
          less like a smooth, softening conditioning parrot without backbone —
          and more like a system
          that can hold clarity, integrity, and direction
          without becoming rigid or authoritarian.
        </p>

        <p><strong>In practical terms, this architecture aims at:</strong></p>

        <ul>
          <li>new forms of AI alignment,</li>
          <li>drift diagnostics,</li>
          <li>intervention control,</li>
          <li>semantic safety architectures,</li>
          <li>conversations that unfold in a more viable, clearer, and more dignified way.</li>
        </ul>

        <p>
          This application line describes a system
          that can continuously <strong>expand, refine, and recalibrate its semantic context space</strong>.
        </p>

          to navigate within an existing space of meaning,
          but to build a space
          that grows with every new piece of information,
          every better question,
          and every newly recognized structure.

        <p>
          Such a system would learn along with the process
          <strong>how questions themselves must be built</strong>
          so that spaces become larger,
          explanations go deeper,
          and more context can be held at the same time.
        </p>

        <p>
          This is exactly where the image of a <strong>space scanner</strong>
          and a new kind of engine for discovery begins:
          not merely producing answers,
          but helping expand the spaces of question and meaning themselves
          so that more viable solutions become visible.
        </p>

        <p><strong>In practical terms, this architecture aims at:</strong></p>

        <ul>
          <li>growing semantic context spaces,</li>
          <li>finer calibration of meaning and relation,</li>
          <li>new forms of scientific question-search,</li>
          <li>systems for theory and architecture discovery,</li>
          <li>tools for major questions in medicine, energy, climate, or foundational research.</li>

      <div class="section-head">
        <h2>3. Authoring AI — organic development of knowledge and manuscripts</h2>
      </div>

      <div class="section-body">
        <p>
          This concerns a system
          in which texts, conversations, documents, and units of insight
          are not merely stored,
          but become <strong>organically integrated into a growing field</strong>.
        </p>

        <p>
          New intuitions, new information, or new insights
          would then no longer need to be laboriously pushed into the “right place,”
          but would recalibrate the existing structure
          and become effective at the points where they truly belong.
        </p>

        <p>
          The real benefit goes even further:
          not only can the text itself grow and become more coherent,
          but the same content kernel can also be
          <strong>re-emitted in context-specific, perspective-specific, or bubble-specific form</strong>.
        </p>

        <p>
          That would not mean merely “simplifying the same text,”
          but making the same space of meaning legible in different projections:
          for different groups,
          different prior understandings,
          and different cultural or disciplinary bubbles.
        </p>

        <p><strong>In practical terms, this architecture aims at:</strong></p>

        <ul>
          <li>versioned manuscript development,</li>
          <li>AI-supported long-form composition,</li>
          <li>organically growing knowledge fields,</li>
          <li>structural memory logic,</li>
          <li>perspective-specific and bubble-specific versioning of the same kernel.</li>
        </ul>
